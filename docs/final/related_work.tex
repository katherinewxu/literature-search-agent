\section{Related Work}

\textbf{LLM agents for literature search.}
PaSa \citep{he2025pasa} trains crawler and selector agents with reinforcement learning to search and
expand citations, and SPAR \citep{shi2025spar} chains reference exploration without training. Deep-research
systems such as OpenScholar \citep{asai2026openscholar} and PaperQA2 \citep{skarlinski2024paperqa2} retrieve
literature to answer questions rather than to find every eligible study. DeepRetrieval
\citep{jiang2025deepretrieval} uses RL to write queries with a recall reward. Benchmarks such as LitSearch
\citep{ajith2024litsearch} and ResearchArena \citep{kang2024researcharena} score topical retrieval; the latter
finds LLM agents trail keyword retrieval. None of these charges for tool calls or applies a systematic review's
eligibility criteria, and none checks whether its gold set is complete.

\textbf{LLMs in systematic-review production.}
LLMs write Boolean queries that favour precision over recall \citep{wang2023chatgptboolean}, screen titles and
abstracts with calibrated thresholds \citep{wang2024zeroshot,guo2024screening,khraisha2024gpt4}, and run
end-to-end pipelines scored against the review's included studies \citep{wang2025trialmind,cao2025ottosr}.
Automated citation searching over OpenAlex reaches a median recall of 35.8\% on 27 reviews
\citep{rajit2025citation}. Small studies report that LLM systems also surface eligible studies the original
authors missed \citep{cao2025ottosr,carrier2026aisr}. We measure this at scale and separate studies the
review's search never retrieved from those its authors screened out.

\textbf{Stopping and recall estimation.}
Technology-assisted review estimates recall to decide when to stop screening a ranked list: knee and target
rules \citep{cormack2016engineering}, hypergeometric tests \citep{callaghan2020stopping}, sampling-based
estimators and certified intervals \citep{li2020stop,lewis2021certifying}, and point-process models
\citep{stevenson2023pointprocess}; \citet{callaghan2024robust} argue such rules need careful evaluation. These
assume a fixed collection and human labels. We estimate recall for an agent's open-ended search with an LLM
screener, so the bound must also absorb the screener's error.

\textbf{Stopping in search agents.}
Technology-assisted review treats \emph{when to stop} as a problem separate from \emph{what to retrieve}
\citep{cormack2016engineering,callaghan2020stopping,lewis2021certifying,stevenson2023pointprocess}, and
interactive IR models searchers' stopping rules explicitly \citep{maxwell2015searching,pirolli1999foraging}.
LLM search agents fold this decision into the policy: the model itself emits STOP. Agents have been reported
to stop early or to plateau when given more tool calls \citep{yen2025lost,liu2025budgetaware}, and their
stopping is decoupled from their own evidence judgments \citep{zhang2026judged}. Forcing a model to continue
instead of terminating improves reasoning \citep[budget forcing;][]{muennighoff2025s1} and web agents
\citep{shen2025thinking}. Separately, simple fixed pipelines often match agentic systems once cost is
controlled \citep{xia2024agentless,kapoor2024agents}, and LLM-written Boolean queries trade recall for
precision \citep{wang2025reassessing}. We bring these threads together for systematic-review search. We compare an
adaptive agent with a fixed LLM query list at matched budget, separate the recall gap into stopping and
inclusion, and test whether rejecting STOP (budget forcing applied to an agent) or prompting for full budget
use closes it.

\textbf{Incomplete relevance judgments.}
Pooled test collections miss relevant documents \citep{zobel1998reliable}, motivating measures robust to
unjudged documents \citep{buckley2004bpref,yilmaz2006infap,sakai2007bpref}; small pools also bias judgments
toward documents that resemble the query \citep{buckley2007bias}. Systematic-review benchmarks inherit this:
their qrels come from the review's own Boolean search \citep{kanoulas2017clef,scells2017testcollection},
single screening misses a median 5\% of eligible studies \citep{waffenschmidt2019single}, and relative recall
pools results across searches \citep{sampson2006relativerecall}. \citet{cormack2018clef} observed that a
whole-MEDLINE system ranked many unassessed records that looked relevant, but did not quantify it. LLMs can fill
judgment holes \citep{thomas2024llmjudge,upadhyay2024umbrela,upadhyay2024patch,abbasiantaeb2024holes}, though
they can be gamed or fooled \citep{clarke2024cantreplace,alaofi2024fooled,soboroff2025dontuse}, and per-topic
classifiers trained on a few human labels can beat prompted judges \citep{gienapp2025topicclassifiers}.
\citet{sahu2026rethinking} argue that human citation lists are not ground truth for literature search. We use
the review's screened-but-excluded records to calibrate an LLM judge, quantify how much of an agent's apparent
error lies outside the review's search, and re-score with pooled relevance.
